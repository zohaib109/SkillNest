# SkillNest pilot product specification

## Who chooses SkillNest and why

An adult university student can arrive with one introductory Python problem, choose a Pakistan-based tutor who teaches that topic at a time and all-in lesson price they understand, and leave with a similar exercise they can try on their own. Recruit tutors who can explain their reasoning in English and guide a learner through debugging. Start with loops, data structures, functions and tracing errors. University maths is the first adjacent subject to test once there is usable supply. Keep existing subject records compatible. Promote the pilot only where actual tutors and openings exist.

The two product bets are finding a suitable *bookable lesson* and finding out *what the learner can now do independently*. Each can fail separately. Use the price set by the tutor, a fixed lesson duration and an explicit currency. Present learning progress as dated examples and learner-reported practice. Teach with analogous examples and invite the student to drive. A tutor should decline requests to complete assessed work or take an exam for the learner.

### Evidence and alternatives

| Candidate | Evidence, feasibility and decision |
| --- | --- |
| Fixed-price help with one learning obstacle | A small think-aloud study documents novices struggling to debug Python systematically. It supports the problem, without estimating commercial demand. Codementor already offers live debugging, with a minimum charge and per-minute billing thereafter. Booking a fixed lesson with a specific objective is feasible with our existing quote and reservation. The hypothesis is that students with tight budgets value predictability and teaching fit. [University research](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/items/c0749ded-a4af-4a35-93f9-cf85aec26272) · [Codementor billing](https://support.codementor.io/en/articles/4221825) |
| Show transfer of learning | Preply already offers summaries and suggested exercises. Our test is narrower. Can a learner solve a fresh version of the original problem, explain the approach, or name where they still need help? A short human-authored task and a private learner response need no recording or model subscription. Tutor time and actual practice completion are the main costs to measure. [Preply Lesson Insights](https://help.preply.com/en/articles/10385861-lesson-insights) |
| Introductory lesson | Offer a normal 30-minute paid lesson where the tutor enables that duration. Same quote, cancellation rules and earnings flow as any other lesson. A sample explanation in a tutor's introduction video costs students nothing and the tutor can reuse it. Free 1:1 demos consume scarce teaching slots, invite repeat signups and require subsidy or unpaid work. Group demos also need scheduling, moderation and sufficient demand. Defer both and test a single private fit question if students still hesitate. [Preply trial commission](https://help.preply.com/en/articles/4171383-preply-commission-model) |
| Tutor confidence in income | Pakistan's central bank identifies cross-border payment friction for freelancers. Give tutors the lesson currency, commission, pending earnings, payout status and any applicable conversion before they commit. Marketplace eligibility and settlement terms require merchant confirmation. Competing on the lowest nominal hourly rate offers little protection against churn. [State Bank of Pakistan](https://archive.sbp.org.pk/reports/half/arFY26/chapter5.pdf) |

## Student and tutor workflows

1. **Explore.** Guests can see approved tutors who explicitly opted in to public display. Existing tutors retain their original verified-student audience by default. Explain exactly which fields and available times become public. Published bio and video must be suitable for public viewing. Moderation checks for contact details or sensitive material before approval. Revocation or loss of approval removes anonymous access on the next request. Public queries return an allowlist. Account identifiers, email, meeting links, bookings, raw weekly rules, blackout dates and moderation notes are private.
2. **Compare and reserve.** Show subject and tutor-authored teaching focus, a duration-specific price in the tutor's currency and a genuine available time, localized on the device. Never compare numeric amounts across currencies. Show an honest empty state when a week is full. An opening is provisional until the server rechecks both participants, price and schedule in the existing hold transaction. Browsing is free. Require a verified student to save, hold or pay. Carry the intended lesson route through sign-in. Clear separate labels for unpaid hold, paid booking and sandbox simulation.
3. **Bring a goal.** At reservation, optionally ask “What would you like to be able to do?” Limit to 300 characters. A returning learner can continue without rewriting the last lesson. Omit uploads, code execution and credentials. The confirmed tutor sees the goal before class. The student can still book with the field empty. Existing bookings render normally.
4. **Teach and follow up.** At the end of a delivered lesson the tutor records one thing practised and one small independent exercise, with an optional HTTPS resource. Aim for under a minute of tutor administration. Students see a dated private record, can mark “Tried independently” or “Still need help”, and can bring it to the next lesson with the same tutor. An omitted response stays unknown. No numerical mastery scores. Only the assigned tutor may publish a recap and only that learner may record their response. Posting content or checking a box never settles money or proves attendance. Keep cancellations and no-shows out of progress history. One recap per booking with retry-safe writes.
5. **Trust.** Label staff moderation “Profile reviewed”. Label experience and qualifications as tutor-provided until a specific claim has supporting evidence, reviewer, review date and revocation path. Introductions are optional and each rating must come from one completed booking. Keep individual credential evidence private and defer document uploads. Public enrollment of minors, guardian accounts and document-based age verification are outside the pilot. An adult invitation and explicit 18+ eligibility attestation at enrollment for both password and OAuth, including existing participants, are release gates. Owner approval covers final terms and eligibility process.
6. **Recover from trouble.** Booking-linked, verified-participant text, role, server timestamp, join authorization, connection/disconnection and reconnect events supply context. Store only the minimum event data and keep access scoped to participants or assigned staff with an audited reason. Skip recording, IP/device fingerprinting and chat retention in analytics. A dropped call alone cannot determine fault. Show both parties the booking timeline, amount and currency, report action, current status, and next step. Reconnect within the same booking. Duplicate events and reports produce one case and one financial outcome.

**Proposed service policy for approval.** Automate expired unpaid holds and acknowledgements. Show an estimate for a straightforward cancellation using terms snapshotted at purchase. Release uncontested refunds only after an approved policy, a supported provider operation and verified settlement. If both parties agree a paid lesson failed, offer rebooking or a refund subject to approved terms. Conflicting reports or unclear evidence pause earnings release and enter a small staff queue. Record the decision, rationale and appeal route. Set refund windows, commission, payout/FX terms, disclosure, retention/deletion periods and jurisdictional requirements with the owner before real transactions. Routine delivery can use local sandbox rules meanwhile.

## Interface and pilot validation

**Review eligibility.** In the sandbox, collect one optional review after both participants confirm the lesson took place. Keep the rating and comment private to the student. Keep support available even after confirmation and separate from the student's opinion of the teaching. For a public pilot, require a genuine paid lesson and a review publication policy covering consent, moderation, appeals and a way for staff to verify delivery when a participant withholds confirmation.

Keep the warm paper background, clear dark text and one primary action per step. Lead with the learning problem and actual tutor information. Tutor cards read in order of teaching fit, lesson length and total, next time, then detail. Show real profiles, honest empty states and precise verbs. Keep illustration secondary. Use native form labels, visible keyboard focus, touch-friendly controls, associated errors, and readable date, timezone and currency text in both themes. Let the learner skip optional fields. Avoid autoplay, urgency timers outside an actual hold, decorative reviews and speculative progress claims.

Start with five adult learners and three tutors in observed, consented sessions, then a small paid pilot only after launch gates. These are suggested recruitment targets, not market evidence. Ask each learner to find an appropriate lesson, explain its total and timezone, complete a similar exercise without help and revisit the next step. Record denominators for discovery to booking, attendance, independent attempts and rebooking within 30 days. Time tutor follow-up and count staff cases per ten lessons. Collect reasons for non-booking and non-return. If students find a slot but fail to attempt practice, shorten the task before expanding subjects. If supply is thin, recruit for the missing topic and time before adding matching algorithms. Keep identifiable learning text out of funnel counts.
